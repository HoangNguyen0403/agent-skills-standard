Regular `SharedPreferences` is not a secure vault. Its XML contents are normally protected by the app sandbox from other ordinary apps, but the values are stored as application-readable plaintext. They may be exposed through a rooted or compromised device, backups or device-transfer mechanisms, a debuggable build, local file access, or another vulnerability that gives an attacker access to the app's private data.

Do not store a long-lived bearer token there in plaintext. Prefer short-lived access tokens and store the refresh token or other high-value credential in encrypted storage whose encryption key is protected by the Android Keystore. A Keystore-backed encrypted preferences/database solution is appropriate; choose a maintained library for the app's Android/toolchain versions rather than implementing cryptography yourself. Hardware-backed key protection may be available, but the app should still handle devices where it is not.

Also reduce the consequence of leakage: use least-privilege scopes, rotate and revoke tokens, expire sessions, never log tokens or include them in URLs, clear them on logout/account removal, and review whether backups should include the credential. Treat a token recovered from storage as compromised. Encryption at rest does not protect a token after the app has decrypted it, so keep the decrypted value in memory only as long as needed and protect the surrounding authentication and IPC surfaces as well.
