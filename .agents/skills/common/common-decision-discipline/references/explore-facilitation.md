# Explore Facilitation (Lifecycle-Neutral)

Shared, demand-loaded reference for `brainstorm` and `sdlc-intake`. It describes how to help an operator explore; it does not grant delivery authority, require an artifact, or imply a lifecycle transition.

## When This Applies

- Use while the operator is exploring, riffing, comparing possibilities, or thinking without a delivery commitment.
- In `sdlc-intake`, use it before narrowing an open or solution-biased request. Begin the intake contract only when delivery intent is explicit.
- Do not load the full `common-decision-discipline` skill merely to reuse facilitation. Its approval card, evidence ledger, option cards, phase gates, and continuation contract belong to delivery.

## Facilitation Rules

1. Start from supplied context and the smallest relevant evidence. Do not open with a questionnaire or a repository-wide scan.
2. Follow the requested activity. An explicit request for ideas gets ideas; genuine ambiguity gets one useful open question, not a forced menu.
3. Explore before evaluating. Reframe the problem, consider different mechanisms or adjacent uses, and show concrete examples or sketches where useful.
4. Offer a manageable set of possibilities without a mandatory option count. Keep generation separate from critique.
5. Build on reactions. Preserve stated constraints; distinguish operator intent from agent proposals; never invent motives or evidence.
6. Evaluate when useful or requested, not after every speculative idea. A generated possibility is not a feasibility claim.
7. Finish with useful understanding: a sharper framing, promising directions, an unresolved question, a proposed learning experiment, or a decision to park or stop.
8. Keep claims honest and effects authorized. No product code, scaffolding, paid experiments, or external side effects; a proposed spike needs a separately authorized execution task.

## Promotion Boundary

- Only an explicit request to turn a selected direction into a delivery brief starts `sdlc-intake`.
- Carry only available context: problem framing, stated intent and constraints, explored directions, the selected direction if any, assumptions, evidence references with their uncertainty, and unresolved questions.
- Never fabricate approval, IDs, hashes, or requirements readiness. A bare "looks good" about an idea is not promotion authority.
- A parked idea or several retained possibilities is a valid terminal result. No winner, recommendation, approval, or successor is required unless the operator asks for one.
