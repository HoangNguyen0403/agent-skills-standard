# Product Customer Discovery Protocol

This reference provides detailed guidance for designing, conducting, and synthesizing non-leading customer discovery interviews and contextual inquiries.

## 1. Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) Inquiry Framework

Customer discovery focuses on understanding customer progress, goals, and struggles rather than gathering feature wish-lists:
- **Core Job**: What fundamental goal is the customer striving to accomplish? (e.g., "Reconcile vendor invoices before month-end financial close").
- **Circumstance / Context**: When, where, and under what constraints does this struggle arise?
- **Current Workarounds & Hacks**: What makeshift solutions (spreadsheets, third-party plug-ins, manual copy-pasting) does the customer employ today?
- **Anxiety & Inertia**: What switching costs, risks, or anxieties prevent them from adopting a new approach?

## 2. Non-Leading Interview Question Design

Interviewers must avoid asking leading, suggestive, or speculative questions that bias the participant:

### Leading Anti-Patterns (BANNED)
- "Wouldn't it be great if this software automatically categorized your receipts?"
- "Do you find the current dashboard confusing?"
- "How much would you pay for an AI-powered assistant that does X?"
- "Would you use a feature that automated your reporting?"

### Non-Leading Evidence-Based Patterns (REQUIRED)
- "Walk me through what happened the last time you prepared the quarterly report."
- "What tools or browser tabs did you have open while performing that task?"
- "When you encountered that error last week, what steps did you take to resolve it?"
- "How much time or money have you spent trying to solve this problem over the past six months?"
- "Who else in your organization had to review or approve that document before it was finalized?"

## 3. Interview Structure & Conduction

1. **Warm-up & Context (5 mins)**:
   - Reconfirm informed consent and recording permission.
   - Explain that there are no right or wrong answers, and that constructive criticism of existing processes is encouraged.
   - Ask about the participant's day-to-day role and immediate responsibilities.
2. **Anchor to Recent Concrete Events (25 mins)**:
   - Identify the most recent time the participant performed the target activity.
   - Anchor the discussion strictly to that specific historical instance.
   - Avoid discussing general opinions or hypothetical future scenarios.
3. **Explore Compensatory Behaviors (15 mins)**:
   - Inquire specifically about manual workarounds, spreadsheets, and custom automations.
   - Ask: "Where in this workflow did you have to pause, wait for someone else, or copy data by hand?"
4. **Wrap-up & Debrief (5 mins)**:
   - Ask: "What is the most frustrating part of that process that we haven't touched on today?"
   - Confirm privacy preferences and follow-up availability.

## 4. Separating Observation from Inference

Use only supplied, appropriately accessible bytes. The quote/action examples below are instructional and are not participant reports.

- **Confirmed**: A literal quote/action shown in an available, de-identified `SRC`, limited to its exact fictional or real session and cohort. Never infer cause, motive, prevalence, recurrence, or impact from a single observation.
- **Inference**: Interpretation (such as hypothesized motivation, generalized problem, or estimated value) with a cited observation, explicit rationale, counterevidence, and limitations.
- If the quote/session cannot be inspected or access is protected/unavailable, do not claim it as confirmed; report a research gap and keep dependent claims unknown, inference, or assumption as warranted.
